How it works

Start it before you go live. Post clips after.

LiveShive attaches to the live edge of the stream, not the start of the VOD, so the first clip is ready about a minute after you press start.

00:00

Paste the link

YouTube, Twitch, Kick, Facebook, a raw .m3u8 or a local file. If you aren't live yet, it waits.

00:20

It listens

Speech is transcribed word by word on your own CPU. Nothing is uploaded.

00:45

It picks moments

Hooks, reactions, loud spikes and gameplay highlights get scored and cut on sentence boundaries.

~01:00

A clip lands

An MP4 with burned-in captions appears in your LiveShive folder, then another, for the whole stream.

Requirements

Runs on a five-year-old laptop too.

On first launch LiveShive reads your RAM and CPU and picks a mode it can keep up with. No graphics card needed. You can switch modes in Settings.

The speech model downloads once the first time you clip, between 140 MB and 1.5 GB depending on the mode. After that LiveShive only needs internet to record the stream.

Questions

Before you install.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC".

New apps without a paid code-signing certificate get this warning. Click More info then Run anyway. The SHA-256 above lets you check the file is the one we published.

Does it upload my stream anywhere?

No. Recording, transcription and editing all happen on your PC. Clips are saved to the LiveShive folder in your user folder.

Which platforms can I clip from?

YouTube, Twitch, Kick, Facebook, Rumble and over a thousand other sites, plus .m3u8, RTMP and video files. Members-only streams work with browser cookies turned on in Settings.

Can I clip someone else's stream?

Technically yes, but only post clips you have permission to use. Most clippers work with the streamer directly.

My laptop is old. Will it keep up?

Light mode uses a smaller speech model, 720p output and the fastest encoder. Clips arrive a little later on a slow CPU, but recording never falls behind.

When is the Mac version coming?

It's being built now. Check the releases page, or watch the GitHub repo to get notified.
